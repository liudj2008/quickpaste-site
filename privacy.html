Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-06

QuickPaste is built to be private. In short: the text you save stays on your device and in your own browser account. We never receive it.

What QuickPaste stores on your device

  • The entries you save (names and text).
  • Your settings: the on/off switch and the list of sites where you turned QuickPaste off (Pro).
  • If you buy Pro, your license key.
  • A simple counter of how many times QuickPaste has filled in text for you. It is shown in settings, stays on your device, and is never sent anywhere.

Browser sync

If you are signed in to Chrome or Edge with browser sync turned on, QuickPaste keeps your entries and license key in step across your own browsers using your browser's built-in extension sync (chrome.storage.sync). That data travels through your Google or Microsoft account under their privacy terms. It never passes through QuickPaste's servers, and we cannot read it. Turn off sync in your browser settings to stop it.

What we do not do

  • No account or login with us.
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising.
  • No selling or sharing of data.
  • No inserting text into password fields, and no reading of password fields.
  • No collecting of the pages you visit, what you type, or your entries.

Permissions and why

  • Access to all websites: to notice what you are typing in a text field, show suggestions, and insert the text you choose. Page content is processed on your device only and is never sent to us.
  • Active tab: when you open the toolbar popup, QuickPaste reads the current site's address so you can switch QuickPaste off for that site (Pro). It is not stored unless you choose to disable the site.
  • Clipboard read/write: some sites only accept a real paste. QuickPaste briefly places your chosen text on the clipboard, pastes it, and restores your previous clipboard. It also copies an entry when you ask it to. Clipboard contents are never stored or transmitted.
  • Storage: to save your entries and settings, and to sync them through your browser account.

Buying and activating Pro: what reaches our server

Payments are handled by Stripe; we never see your card number. When you pay, Stripe gives us your email address and the order and payment reference. We store them in our license database (hosted on Amazon Web Services) and use the email only to send you your license key and to handle support, refunds and disputes.

When you activate a Pro license key, and again every few days to confirm it is still valid, QuickPaste sends our license server the license key, a random device ID, and a device label made of the browser and platform, such as "QuickPaste Chrome on MacIntel". If you choose to give the device a name (for example "Work laptop"), that name is sent too. We also record when each activation happened and how many times a key has been activated. This is used only to check the key, to enforce the per-key device limit, and to limit repeated activations of the same key. No entries and no browsing data are sent. If you never activate Pro, QuickPaste makes no network requests.

Managing your devices. If your key is full because a computer was lost or wiped, you can enter your purchase email on our device page. If that email has a license, we send it one link that works for 30 minutes and lists the key's devices (name, label and dates; the key itself is masked) so you can remove one. To limit abuse we briefly keep the email address you entered, and a secure hash of the link, for up to a few hours before they are deleted automatically.

We keep license records for as long as the license exists, and for as long as needed for tax, refund and fraud-prevention purposes.

Deleting your data

Delete entries in QuickPaste's settings, or remove the extension to erase everything it stored on that device. Synced copies are removed by deleting the entries on any synced browser, or through your browser account's sync controls. To have your email and license record deleted from our license database, contact us below (an active license that is deleted will stop working).
